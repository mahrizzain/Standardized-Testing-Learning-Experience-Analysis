# The Impact of Standardized Testing on Students' Learning Experience

**Original survey research on how high school students actually experience standardized testing — mental health, motivation, fairness, and whether tests prepare them for what comes next.**

Independent research paper by Mahriz Zain (April 2024), mentored by **Dr. Suman Basuroy**.

## Research Question

How does standardized testing impact students' learning experience?

Most published literature on standardized testing argues from theory or secondary data, and it contradicts itself — some studies find testing benefits learning (retrieval practice, feedback, motivation), others find stress and narrowed instruction. This study goes to the primary source the literature largely skips: **students themselves**.

## Methods

- **Instrument:** Original 15-question survey built from the contested factors in the literature — mental health, motivation, fairness, and attitudes toward tests and the education system. Questions were ordered from self → peers → administrators to build from what respondents know best.
- **Measurement:** Closed-ended questions using a **multichotomous semantic differential scale (1–7)** with defined anchors and a built-in vocabulary dictionary, so perceptions could be quantified without flattening them into vague answer choices.
- **Sample:** 70 high school respondents, primarily Irving ISD and the Dallas–Fort Worth area (52.9% Hispanic, 31.4% Asian, 7.1% Black), collected over ~3 weeks via Google Forms; analysis in Google Sheets.
- **Analysis:** Response patterns compared factor-by-factor against the claims in the literature review, checking for consistency across factors.

## Key Findings

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| System reform | **87.2%** of students agreed the current teaching system needs to be reformed to improve learning — **64.2% strongly agreed** |
| Mental health | Students consistently reported standardized tests negatively affect student mental health |
| Alternatives | Students believed alternative learning systems would be more effective than the current testing system |
| Preparation | Students reported standardized tests do **not** adequately prepare them for future education |
| Literature check | Results aligned with the negative-impact literature; the positive-effects arguments (e.g., retrieval practice) were not supported strongly enough by student responses to sustain that narrative |

## Limitations

- n = 70 from one metro area — nationally suggestive (testing mandates are federal, via No Child Left Behind), not nationally representative.
- Few Caucasian respondents (5.7%), so socioeconomic/demographic comparisons across groups could not be tested — flagged as the main direction for future research.
- Self-reported perceptions measure the *experienced* impact of testing, not test-score outcomes.
